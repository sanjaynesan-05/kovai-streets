# 2. CRITICAL BUG FIXES

These issues must be addressed before adding large new features.

## 2.1 Flyover and bridge collision bug

### Current problem
The player cannot drive underneath flyovers and bridges. The underside of the bridge behaves like a solid wall, blocking vehicles and players even when they are travelling on the road underneath.

### Required result
- Vehicles must be able to pass beneath flyovers.
- Pedestrians must be able to walk beneath bridges.
- The bridge deck must remain physically solid from above.
- Support pillars must retain collision.
- Road surfaces beneath the bridge must remain accessible.
- Collision must match the actual geometry of the structure.

### Implementation requirements
- Inspect the current collision mesh and physics implementation.
- Separate bridge deck, pillars, road, and surrounding collision geometry.
- Use simplified collision meshes where appropriate.
- Avoid oversized bounding boxes that block the entire area beneath the flyover.
- Verify collision layers and object groups.
- Check vehicle height and clearance.
- Test driving underneath from multiple directions.

Do not simply disable all bridge collisions.

## 2.2 Two-wheeler passenger visibility bug

### Current problem
When the player picks up a friend or NPC on a motorcycle or scooter, the passenger disappears during the ride but becomes visible again after the player exits the vehicle.

### Required result
The passenger must remain visible throughout the ride.

### Implementation requirements
- Inspect the passenger attachment and vehicle parenting logic.
- Correct character visibility and rendering state.
- Attach the passenger to a suitable pillion position.
- Implement an appropriate passenger riding animation.
- Keep the passenger correctly positioned during acceleration, braking, and turning.
- Prevent clipping through the rider or vehicle.
- Restore the passenger's normal character state when dismounting.
- Ensure the passenger cannot accidentally detach or disappear during camera changes.

Test with every supported two-wheeler.

# 10. SMART GANG AI AND TERRITORY SYSTEM

### Current problem
Rival gang members attack the player immediately whenever they see them.

This makes the world feel artificial.

### Required behavior

Implement a gang relationship and hostility system.

**Stage 1 — Neutral**
- Gang members ignore the player.
- They perform normal idle activities.
- They interact with nearby NPCs.
- They remain within their territory.

**Stage 2 — Suspicious**
- Gang members notice the player.
- They observe the player.
- They may approach or follow at a distance.
- They react to suspicious behavior.

**Stage 3 — Warning**
- Gang members verbally warn the player.
- They communicate that the player is entering their territory.
- They may ask the player to leave.

**Stage 4 — Hostile**
- Gang members attack if the player provokes them.
- They defend themselves if attacked.
- They respond to attacks on their allies.
- They may coordinate with nearby gang members.

**Stage 5 — Escalation**
- Repeated attacks increase hostility.
- Reinforcements may arrive.
- The gang may remember previous confrontations.
- Territory danger increases temporarily.

**Stage 6 — Cooldown**
- Gang hostility should decrease after the player leaves the area.
- Enemies should stop chasing indefinitely.
- NPCs should eventually return to normal behavior.

### Important wanted-level rule

Gang hostility must not automatically make the player wanted.

Police attention should depend on witnessed crimes, reported incidents, evidence, and other appropriate gameplay conditions.

### Technical requirements

Use:
- Finite-state machines or behavior trees.
- Detection ranges.
- Line-of-sight checks.
- Territory zones.
- Gang relationship values.
- Memory of recent incidents.
- Cooldown timers.
- Group coordination.

Avoid giving every gang member expensive continuous AI processing.
